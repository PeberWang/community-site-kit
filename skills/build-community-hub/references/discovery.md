# Discovery interview

Use this sequence adaptively. Ask one question at a time; skip what existing evidence answers.

## 1. Purpose and first loop

- Who is the community, and what repeated problem hurts today?
- What do people currently use? What fails in that workaround?
- What is the smallest two-week loop that would prove usefulness?
- What should this site explicitly not become?

## 2. People and power

- Who reads, contributes, reviews, moderates, operates, funds, and can shut it down?
- Who may be harmed or excluded?
- Is real-name, pseudonymous, or anonymous participation appropriate?
- Who makes the final call in a dispute, and how can someone appeal?

## 3. Content lifecycle

- Which knowledge evolves over time? Which content expires? Which is casual talk?
- What context makes a contribution useful rather than just another file?
- Who may correct, withdraw, archive, or restore content?
- What attribution and copyright permission is required?

## 4. Privacy and models

- List personal, sensitive, confidential, copyrighted, and location data.
- Which data may be public, member-only, administrator-only, or never collected?
- Which data may be sent to an external model or OCR provider?
- State retention and deletion expectations for each class.

## 5. Sustainability

- Who can spend one hour every week maintaining this?
- What is the monthly budget ceiling?
- How will leadership succession work?
- How will the community export data or leave a provider?

## Required artifacts

In `docs/product_brief.md`, record users, problems, scenarios, selected modules, non-goals, success signals, assumptions, unknowns, rollout, and owners.

In `docs/privacy_inventory.md`, record each data class, source, purpose, visibility, model access, storage, retention, deletion method, and owner. Mark unknowns as blockers rather than guessing.
